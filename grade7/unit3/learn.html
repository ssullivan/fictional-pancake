Measuring Circles

Short lessons you can play with. Find the radius and diameter, discover π, roll wheels, cut circles into wedges to find their area, and decide when you need the circumference or the area, then check yourself. When you’re ready, practice in Wheel Works.

For grown-ups

How it works

Five chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

Where π comes from

Students first see that every circle’s circumference divided by its diameter is the same number, a bit more than 3, before calling it π. The circumference formula, C = πd, follows from that, and a rolling wheel shows it in action.

Where πr² comes from

A circle cut into thin wedges and laid out point up, point down makes a shape close to a parallelogram half the circumference long and one radius tall. That’s πr × r. Slide the number of wedges to watch the shape straighten out.

Exact and estimated

Answers are written in terms of π (12π cm), which is exact, and estimated with 3.14 where a real measurement is needed. Quick checks type the number in front of π.

Practice

Each chapter ends with a link to the matching station in Wheel Works, the practice game for this unit. Progress is saved on this device.

Not covered
