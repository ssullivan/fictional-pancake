For grown-ups

How a round works

Each station deals 8 fresh problems (the Master Key deals 10). A right answer on the first try earns 10 points. After a wrong answer, the game names the likely mistake and gives one more try for 5 points. A question with only two choices (is it a solution?) gets one try.

Typing negative numbers

Answers can be negative, so the answer box opens the full keyboard, which has a minus sign. Either a hyphen or a true minus sign works.

Two kinds of equations

IM contrasts px + q = r (some equal parts plus one more) with p(x + q) = r (equal groups of two things). Tape diagrams and hangers show the difference, and the game asks students to match stories and diagrams to the right kind before solving.

Numbers you can do in your head

Solutions are whole numbers from −10 to 10, coefficients are small, and budgets and goals use friendly amounts, so the challenge is the reasoning, not the arithmetic.

Mistakes it catches

Undoing in the wrong order, dividing only part of p(x + q), the wrong sign when undoing a subtraction, forgetting to turn an inequality around when dividing by a negative, a hollow circle read as filled, rounding the wrong way for “most” and “fewest,” distributing to only the first term, and subtracting only part of a group.

Not covered
