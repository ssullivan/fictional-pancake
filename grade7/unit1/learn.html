For grown-ups

How it works

Seven chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

Multiply, don’t add

The big idea of the unit is that a scaled copy multiplies every length by the same number, the scale factor. The most common mistake is adding the same amount to every side, so the first chapters compare scaled copies with stretched ones and with copies made by adding, side by side on a grid.

Lengths, angles, and area

Angles don’t change in a scaled copy, and area changes by the scale factor times itself. Both surprise students, so the pages show them: angle measures that stay put as a shape grows, and a scaled-up shape cut into copies of the original.

Scales

A scale drawing is a scaled copy of something real. A scale with units (1 cm to 5 m) and one without (1 to 500) say the same thing once both lengths are in the same unit. Every scale in the unit uses friendly numbers, so the challenge is choosing to multiply or divide.

Practice
