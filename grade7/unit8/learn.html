Probability & Sampling

Short lessons you can play with. Place events from impossible to certain, count outcomes, spin a spinner again and again, list two-step outcomes in tables and trees, pick fair samples, and use samples to estimate and compare, then check yourself. When you’re ready, practice in Carnival of Chance.

For grown-ups

How it works

Five chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

Chance you can repeat

The spinner and the samples use a fixed sequence of random numbers, so they come out the same each time the page opens. That keeps the pages testable while still showing what students see in class: a few tries can be far off, and many tries settle near the probability.

Samples

The sampling chapters build IM’s main ideas: random samples are the fairest, a sample’s share or mean estimates the population’s, samples vary, and a gap between two samples’ means matters when it’s more than about 2 MADs.

Practice

Each chapter ends with a link to the matching station in Carnival of Chance, the practice game for this unit. Progress is saved on this device.

Not covered

Lesson 10 (designing simulations) and Lesson 20 (a class memory test) are hands-on experiments, so they work better in class.
