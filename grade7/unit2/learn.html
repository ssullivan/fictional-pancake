Proportional Relationships

Short lessons you can play with. Find the constant of proportionality in tables, write equations like y = kx, test whether a relationship is proportional, read graphs through (0, 0), and see one relationship four ways, then check yourself. When you’re ready, practice in Food Truck Rally.

For grown-ups

How it works

Five chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

One number ties it together

The constant of proportionality is the unit’s big idea: the number in every row of a table, the k in y = kx, and the height of the graph at x = 1. Each chapter finds it a different way, and the last one puts all four representations side by side.

Not everything is proportional

Students test tables by dividing each y by its x, and learn to spot what breaks proportionality: a fee added on, an age difference, a squared side. On a graph, the line must go through (0, 0).

Going back

Every proportional relationship works in two directions, and the constants are reciprocals: 6 pages per minute is 1/6 of a minute per page. That shows up in tables, in the second equation, and when a graph’s axes are swapped.
