For grown-ups

How a round works

Each station deals 8 fresh problems (Opening Night deals 10). A right answer on the first try earns 10 points. After a wrong answer, the game names the likely mistake and gives one more try for 5 points. A question with only two choices (which neighborhood is more crowded?) gets one try.

Putting it all together

This optional unit uses the year’s math in one setting: proportional relationships to scale recipes (Units 2 and 4), percents for markups (Unit 4), scale drawings for the floor plan (Unit 1), rates for how crowded a neighborhood is, circumference for a trundle wheel (Unit 3), percent error (Unit 4), and an equation for the race route (Unit 6).

Numbers you can do in your head

Recipe amounts are halves, thirds, and quarters of a cup, prices are friendly, and the course’s pieces come out even, so the challenge is choosing the right tool.

Mistakes it catches

Adding to scale a recipe instead of multiplying, the markup without the cost, dividing the wrong way for a unit price, forgetting to subtract costs for profit, a scaled area without both sides scaled, more people taken for more crowded, and dividing before taking off what’s already done.

Not covered

Lesson 6 (Fermi problems), Lesson 11 (building a trundle wheel), and Lesson 13 (designing a 5K course) are open-ended or hands-on, so they work better in class than as scored problems.
