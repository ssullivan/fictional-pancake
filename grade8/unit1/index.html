For grown-ups

How a round works

Each station deals 8 fresh problems (Launch Day deals 10). A right answer on the first try earns 10 points. After a wrong answer, the game names the likely mistake and gives one more try for 5 points. A question with only two choices (is it congruent?) gets one try.

The "Show me" hint

Hints say how to check a move one corner at a time (count the squares, turn the segment from the center, measure the distance to the mirror line), give the coordinate rule, and name the angle pair that makes two angles equal or add to 180°. After each problem the picture shows the move itself: the arrow, the center of rotation, or the line of reflection.

Numbers you can do in your head

Shapes sit on a grid with whole-number corners between −7 and 7, translations are whole squares, rotations are quarter and half turns around a grid point, and reflections are over the axes or a grid line. Angles are multiples of 5 degrees, so the challenge is the geometry, not the arithmetic.

Mistakes it catches

Turning the wrong way, mixing up the x-axis and the y-axis, swapping the across and up-and-down moves, sliding a shape across a mirror line instead of flipping it, doing two moves in the wrong order, calling a scaled or stretched copy congruent, matching the wrong corners of congruent polygons, taking supplementary angles for equal ones (and the other way round), and adding a triangle’s angles to 360°.

Not covered

Lesson 17 (Rotate and Tessellate) is a design project where students make their own tessellations, so it works better in class than as scored problems.
