Rigid Transformations & Congruence

Short lessons you can play with. Slide, turn, and flip shapes on grids and coordinate planes, write the rules, see what stays the same, test for congruence, and find the angles along parallel lines and inside triangles, then check yourself. When you’re ready, practice in Sprite Studio.

For grown-ups

How it works

Eight chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

One corner at a time

Every move is checked the same way: follow one corner. A translation moves it a set number of squares across and up, a rotation turns the segment from the center to it, and a reflection puts it just as far on the other side of the line. The pictures draw that arrow, segment, or line, so students can see the move, not just its result.

Rules on the coordinate plane

With coordinates, each move becomes a rule: a translation adds to x and y, a reflection over an axis changes one sign, and a rotation around the origin swaps the coordinates and changes a sign. The rules are written in math type, like (x, y) → (−y, x).

Congruence and angles

Rigid moves keep every length and angle, so congruent shapes are the ones some moves fit exactly together. The same idea explains the angles: a translation matches corresponding angles, a half turn matches alternate interior angles, and those prove that a triangle’s angles add to 180°.
