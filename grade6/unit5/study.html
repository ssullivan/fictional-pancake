Bundle: ten of a place make one of the place to its left: 10 hundredths = 1 tenth.

Regroup: unbundle one of a place into ten of the next place to subtract.

Product, factor: in 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08, 0.08 is the product.

Quotient, divisor: in 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4, 2.4 is the quotient and 5 the divisor.

Estimate first: 2.5 × 0.12 is about 2.5 × 0.1 = 0.25.

Check: quotient × divisor = dividend, and a difference plus what you took away gives back the start.

Part A · Lessons 1–4

Adding and subtracting decimals

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • Add tenths to tenths and hundredths to hundredths. Ten hundredths bundle into 1 tenth, and ten tenths into 1 one.
  • In columns, line up the decimal points, so each digit sits over the same place.
  • Fill in zeros so the numbers have the same number of places: 2 is 2.00.
  • To subtract a bigger digit, regroup: 1 one is 10 tenths, and 1 tenth is 10 hundredths.

Example

Find 0.47 + 0.38, and 2 − 0.75.

0.47+ 0.380.85
2.00− 0.751.25

7 + 8 = 15 hundredths: 1 tenth and 5 hundredths. For 2 − 0.75, write 2 as 2.00 and regroup.

Watch out: 0.75 forgets the bundled tenth, and 2 − 0.75 = 0.55 lines the 2 up with the tenths. Line up the decimal points.

Part B · Lessons 5–6

Where the decimal point goes

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • Write the decimals as fractions: 0.3 × 0.2 = 3/10 × 2/10 = 6/100 = 0.06.
  • Tenths × tenths make hundredths; tenths × hundredths make thousandths. So the product can be smaller than both factors.
  • Quick way: multiply as if there were no decimal points, then put back as many decimal places as the factors have together.
  • 2.5 × 0.12: 25 × 12 = 300, and 1 + 2 = 3 places: 0.300, which is 0.3.

Example

Find 0.3 × 0.2.

The rectangle covers 3 × 2 = 6 of the 100 small squares: 0.06.

Watch out: 0.4 × 0.2 is 0.08, not 0.8. Count the places: 1 + 1 = 2.

Part C · Lessons 7–8

Area diagrams and grids

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • A 1 meter by 1 meter square cut into 100 small squares shows hundredths: each small square is 0.01 square meter.
  • A rectangle 0.6 m by 0.3 m covers 6 × 3 = 18 small squares: 0.18 square meter.
  • To multiply by a number like 1.5, split it into easy parts (1 and 0.5), find each partial product, and add.
  • Whole numbers split the same way: 3.2 × 4 = 3 × 4 + 0.2 × 4 = 12.8.

Example

Grapes cost $2.40 a pound. How much do 1.5 pounds cost?

1 pound: $2.40. Half a pound: $1.20. Together: $2.40 + $1.20 = $3.60.

Watch out: $2.40 + 1.5 adds instead of multiplying. And 0.4 × 0.7 is 0.28: not 28, and not 0.028.

Part D · Lessons 9–11

Dividing whole numbers

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • Partial quotients: take away groups of the divisor in easy chunks (10 groups, 20, 100) until nothing is left, then add up the groups.
  • Long division does the same thing one place at a time.
  • When something is left over, keep going: 1 one is 10 tenths, so divide the tenths, then the hundredths.
  • Check: quotient × divisor = dividend.

Example

156 cookies go in boxes of 12. And 5 friends share $12.

156− 12010 × 1236− 363 × 12013 boxes
12.0− 10.02 × 52.0− 2.00.4 × 50.0$2.40 each

Each friend gets $2 with $2 left over; $2 is 20 tenths, and 20 ÷ 5 = 4 tenths more.

Watch out: 2 left over doesn’t make 2.2. Keep dividing: the 2 ones left are 20 tenths, and 20 tenths ÷ 5 = 0.4.

Part E · Lessons 12–13

Dividing decimals

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • Multiplying both numbers in a division by the same number doesn’t change the quotient: 1.8 ÷ 0.3 = 18 ÷ 3 = 6.
  • So multiply both by 10 or 100 until the divisor is a whole number, then divide.
  • Dividing a decimal by a whole number works like whole numbers; the decimal point stays in its place: 7.2 ÷ 4 = 1.8.

Example

How many 0.25-liter cups can be filled from 3 liters of juice?

division
3 ÷ 0.25
30 ÷ 2.5both × 10
300 ÷ 25both × 100

300 ÷ 25 = 12 cups. Check: 12 × 0.25 = 3.

Watch out: multiply both numbers by the same thing. 30 ÷ 25 = 1.2 multiplies only the 3.

Part F · Lesson 14

Putting it together

Learn chapter →

Know it

  • Real problems take a few steps: multiply for more than one of something, add up the order, then subtract to make change.
  • Line up the decimal points every time you add or subtract.
  • Estimate first, so you can tell if the answer makes sense: 3 tacos at about $2 is about $6.

Example

Lin buys 3 tacos at $2.25 each and a lemonade for $1.40, and pays with a $10 bill. How much change?

3 tacos3 × $2.25 = $6.75
lemonade$1.40
total$6.75 + $1.40 = $8.15
change$10.00 − $8.15 = $1.85

Watch out: $8.15 is the total, not the change. And 3 tacos cost 3 × $2.25, not $2.25.
