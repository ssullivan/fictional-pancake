For grown-ups

How it works

Six chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try.

An optional unit that uses the whole year

IM’s Unit 9 has no new standards: it puts the year’s math to work. Shrinking the world to a class uses ratios and rates (Units 2 and 3). Cutting a rectangle into squares finds the greatest common factor (Unit 7) and shows why a fraction like 16/5 is 3 1/5. Comparing votes, supermajorities, and turnout use percents (Unit 3). Sharing representatives uses “people per representative,” a unit rate.

Fair comparisons

The voting chapters keep coming back to one idea: groups of different sizes are compared by their shares, not their counts. A bigger class can have more yes votes and be less in favor, and 80% of the voters can be 4% of the town.

No single fairest rule

The same ranked ballots can pick three different winners: by plurality, by a runoff, and by instant runoff points. IM calls the points count (3, 2, 1, 0 for four choices) “instant runoff,” and these pages do too; elsewhere it’s often called a Borda count.

Practice

Each chapter ends with a link to the matching station in Town Hall, the practice game for this unit. Progress is saved on this device.

Not covered
