Dividing Fractions

Short lessons you can play with. Count groups of a fraction with pattern blocks and tape diagrams, find how much is in one group, see why dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal, and use it for lengths, areas, and volumes, then check yourself. When you’re ready, practice in Fraction Workshop.

For grown-ups

How it works

Eight chapters follow the unit’s lessons in order. Each step shows one idea with something to change, then a quick check. A wrong answer names the likely mistake and gives one more try. Fraction answers can be typed as 14/3, 4 2/3, or a close decimal.

The tools from class

Pattern blocks (a hexagon is 1 whole), tape diagrams cut into equal parts with the groups bracketed, double number lines when there are two amounts to track, rectangles tiled in small squares, and boxes filled with small cubes. Every division comes with the multiplication that checks it.

Two questions behind every division

“How many groups?” (how many ¾-cup scoops are in 6 cups) and “How much in each group?” (5 cups fill ⅔ of a pitcher, so how much fills the whole pitcher). Most mistakes come from mixing up the two, so the steps keep asking what the groups are and what is known.

Other ways it’s shown

Besides the tape diagrams from class: hops along a ruler, rewriting both fractions with the same denominator (6 eighths ÷ 1 eighth is 6), estimating first (is the answer more or less than 1?), and multiplying back to check. “Flip and multiply” is built up from the pictures (multiply by the denominator, then divide by the numerator) rather than handed over as a rule.

Practice

Each chapter ends with a link to the matching station in Fraction Workshop, the practice game for this unit. Progress is saved on this device.
