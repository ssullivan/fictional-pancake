Part A · Lessons 1–3

Reasoning to find area

Know it

  • Area is how many unit squares cover a flat shape, with no gaps and no overlaps.
  • Cut a shape into pieces and move them around: the area stays the same.
  • Two ways to find an odd shape’s area: break it into pieces and add, or surround it with a rectangle and subtract the extra.
  • Area is in square units: cm², in², ft², m².

Example

Break it apart: 6 × 2 = 12 and 2 × 3 = 6, so 12 + 6 = 18.

Or surround it with a 6 × 5 rectangle and subtract the empty corner: 30 − 4 × 3 = 30 − 12 = 18 square units.

Watch out: count squares, not the lines around the outside. And when you break a shape apart, don’t let the pieces overlap, or you count some squares twice.

Part B · Lessons 4–6

Parallelograms

Know it

  • A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides.
  • Cut the triangle off one end and slide it to the other: you get a rectangle. So Area = base × height.
  • The base can be any side. The height is the straight-up distance from the base to the opposite side. It meets the base at a right angle (the little square).
  • The slanted side is not the height.
  • The height can be drawn outside the shape. Extend the base with a dashed line.

Example

A = b × h = 6 × 4 = 24 cm².

The 5 cm side is slanted, so it isn’t used.

Watch out: 6 × 5 = 30 is wrong. It uses the slanted side instead of the height.

Part C · Lessons 7–10

Triangles

Know it

  • Two copies of any triangle fit together into a parallelogram. So a triangle is half of base × height: Area = ½ × b × h (or b × h ÷ 2).
  • The height goes from the corner opposite the base straight down to the base, at a right angle.
  • In a right triangle, the two sides that make the square corner are the base and the height.
  • In a wide (obtuse) triangle, the height may fall outside. Extend the base to meet it.
  • Triangles with the same base and height have the same area, whatever their shape.

Example

A = ½ × 10 × 6 = ½ × 60 = 30 m².

Watch out: don’t forget the ½. 10 × 6 = 60 is the area of the whole parallelogram, twice the triangle.

Part D · Lesson 11

Polygons

Know it

  • A polygon is a closed, flat shape with straight sides that meet only at their ends (vertices).
  • To find its area, break it into rectangles and triangles and add their areas.
  • Or surround it with a rectangle and subtract the corners you don’t need.
  • For a shape with a hole, find the big area and subtract the hole.

Example

Rectangle: 6 × 4 = 24. Each triangle: ½ × 2 × 4 = 4.

Total: 24 + 4 + 4 = 32 square units.

Watch out: 10 × 4 = 40 counts space outside the trapezoid, and 6 × 4 = 24 leaves out the two triangles.

Part E · Lessons 12–15

Polyhedra, nets, and surface area

Know it

  • A polyhedron is a closed 3D shape made of flat polygon faces. Faces meet at edges, and edges meet at vertices (corners). Cylinders, cones, and spheres are not polyhedra because they have curved surfaces.
  • A prism has two matching, parallel bases joined by rectangles. A pyramid has one base and triangles that meet at a point. Both are named for their base: triangular prism, square pyramid, …
  • A net is a flat pattern that folds up into the solid: one piece for each face.
  • Surface area = the total area of all the faces = the area of the net, in square units.
ShapeFacesEdgesVertices
Cube / rectangular prism6128

Example

Surface area of a box 5 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm. Unfold it into a net:

Two faces of each kind: 2 × 15 + 2 × 10 + 2 × 6
= 30 + 20 + 12 = 62 cm².

Watch out: a box has 6 faces, so add all of them, not just the 3 you can see. And 5 × 3 × 2 = 30 is the box’s volume (cubic units), not its surface area.

Part F · Lessons 16–18

Surface area vs. volume, squares and cubes

Know it

  • Surface area is how much it takes to cover the outside: square units (cm²). Volume is how much space is inside: cubic units (cm³).
  • An exponent says how many times to multiply a number by itself: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25 (“5 squared”), 5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125 (“5 cubed”).
  • A square with side s: Area = s².
  • A cube with edge s: Volume = s³ and Surface area = 6 × s² (six square faces).

Example

Each face: 4² = 16 cm². Six faces: 6 × 16 = 96 cm².

Volume: 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 cm³.

Watch out: 4² means 4 × 4 = 16, not 4 × 2 = 8. And 4³ is 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, not 4 × 3 = 12.
