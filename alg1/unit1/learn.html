Lesson 1: Getting to Know You

The warm-up’s four questions about potato chips sort two ways: answers that are numbers or categories, and one answer or answers that vary. A statistical question is one you answer by collecting data that vary; data have variability when not all the values are the same. Data are numerical when they come from measuring or counting, and categorical when they’re characteristics. Numbers that are labels, like zip codes and jersey numbers, are categorical: their average means nothing.

The class survey

In class, students write survey questions and ask 15 classmates. Here, the page asks for them: pick a question and see 15 answers, whether they vary, and what they say. It’s worth running a real survey at home too: ask everyone in the family the same few questions and sort them.
